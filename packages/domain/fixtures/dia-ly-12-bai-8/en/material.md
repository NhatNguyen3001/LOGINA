## 2. Employment structure by economic sector

{{para p8-1}}
Vietnam's employment structure by economic sector has shifted in a positive direction. The share of workers in agriculture, forestry and fisheries has fallen; the share in industry and construction and in services has risen (Table 8.1).

{{table 8.1}}
**Table 8.1. Employed workers in Vietnam by economic sector, 2010–2021 (%)**

| Economic sector | 2010 | 2021 |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture, forestry and fisheries | 49.5 | 29.1 |
| Industry and construction | 21.0 | 33.1 |
| Services | 29.5 | 37.8 |
| Total | 100.0 | 100.0 |

*Source: General Statistics Office of Vietnam (rounded figures).*

{{para p8-2}}
Even so, agriculture, forestry and fisheries still account for a large share of workers. In 2021, nearly one in three workers was employed in this sector.

{{para p8-3}}
Several factors drive this shift. Industrialization and modernization create more jobs in industry and services. Urbanization expands cities and industrial zones, drawing workers from rural areas. International economic integration and foreign investment bring more factories and businesses into operation. Mechanization in agriculture reduces the number of workers needed for each harvest.

{{page 9}}

{{para p9-1}}
Workforce quality keeps improving thanks to achievements in education and training. The share of trained workers is rising, but it is still low compared with what many new industries need.

{{para p9-2}}
For rural workers, especially young people, there are two common paths to a job. The first is to move to a city or an industrial zone to work in a factory. This path pays more steadily than farming, but workers live far from their families, living costs are high, and many jobs require skills they have not been trained for.

{{para p9-3}}
The second is to stay in their home area and work in services, a craft village, or farming combined with a side job. This path keeps them close to family and community, but income is often lower and irregular, with fewer chances to learn a trade or move up.

{{para p9-4}}
Creating suitable jobs for rural youth therefore depends on vocational training, growing local businesses, and better living conditions in both rural and urban areas.

{{secondary}}
> **Further reading (secondary source, 2015).** A 2015 report put the share of workers at about 44.0% in agriculture, forestry and fisheries, 22.8% in industry and construction, and 33.2% in services.
